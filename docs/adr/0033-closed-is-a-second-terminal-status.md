# Closed is a second terminal status: a Ticket can be dropped at an Interrupt without merging

Until issue #154 (https://github.com/BillyLuisNeedham/agents-console/issues/154) a Ticket ended one way: `done`, with its work merged. When the direction changed, a checkpointed Ticket could become obsolete, and the only ways out of its Interrupt were Resume and Keep talking. Both cost an Attempt and both end `done`. In the og-review pool, clearing nine superseded review Tickets meant resuming each with "write done, make no commits": nine Attempts spent on nothing, and nine cards that read as finished work nobody did.

**Close is an answer, and `closed` is a status.** A checkpoint, merge-conflict or deadlock Interrupt now offers Close beside Resume, with an optional closing note. The engine writes `status=closed` into the Ticket's marker, records the answer and its note on the Ticket log, and appends the note to the Ticket file under `## Close note` (`## Close note, from the Steward` when the Steward closed it), the way a Resume note is appended. Close goes through the queued-answer path like every other answer (ADR-0004), so it lands at the super-step boundary, or at once on an idle pool. Config and crash Interrupts do not offer it: they are for fixing the environment and resuming.

**Close never merges.** Every branch and worktree the Ticket owns, its solo branch and every Attempt branch, is discarded the way a losing Attempt's are, and its Held pane is closed. Two kinds of work are not the engine's to throw away, and Close leaves them in place and says so on the log. A lone Ticket that ran in the Pool checkout has its commits on the pool branch already, and the engine never resets them. An enlisted Ticket's pane, directory and branch are the operator's (ADR-0021).

**A closed blocker never unblocks.** `closed` does not satisfy blocked-by. A Ticket waiting on one gets the existing deadlock Interrupt, whose reason names the closed blocker, and that Interrupt offers Close too. So a cascade is one decision per dependent and never automatic. The one exception is the grading Tickets the engine wrote for the closed Ticket (its grader and head-to-head tickets). They close with it, because nobody chose them and they have nothing left to grade.

**`closed` counts as finished.** A run whose remaining Tickets are all closed ends normally rather than `stalled`. The Review gate does not wait on closed Tickets, a Review reject does not reopen one, and the Merge hold and Merge queue ignore them. A parent Conversation is told its spawned Ticket was closed, with the note, as it is told of done and checkpoint.

**The Steward may Close only when the operator allows it.** A Pool setting, "Steward may Close checkpoints" (`steward.mayClose` in `console.json`), is off by default. While it is off, the Steward's teaching lists no close command and the engine refuses one. While it is on, the Steward may Close a checkpoint or merge conflict with a note, recorded as the Steward's and counted against its Steward budget. Closing a deadlocked dependent stays the operator's. ADR-0030 rejected a pool setting for push rights because the engine could not enforce it. This setting the engine enforces when the answer arrives, so the setting is the honest place for it.

**Rejected:**
- **Close as `done` without a merge.** No new status, so nothing downstream changes. But the board, the Spawn ledger and every dependent would read dropped work as finished, which is the lie the issue is about, and dependents would silently start on work that never happened.
- **Calling it Discard.** Discard already means "gone for good" for a Held spawn. A closed Ticket stays visible with its log, so it needed its own word.
- **Cascading Close to dependents automatically.** A dependent may still make sense with a different blocker, or with none. The deadlock Interrupt asks that question once per Ticket at the cost of one click.

**Consequences:** the marker grammar gains a fifth status. That is part of the on-disk contract, so a server from before this change cannot load a Pool that holds a closed Ticket: its marker parser refuses unknown statuses and fails the whole Pool load. Rolling a Pool back across this change means editing those markers by hand first, and letting any queued Close drain beforehand: an older server reads a queued Close as a Resume. The Rust port of the engine (issue #162) inherits `closed` as part of the contract it must match. Reopening a closed Ticket, and closing a `ready` Ticket that never started (other than one stopped at a deadlock), are out of scope.
